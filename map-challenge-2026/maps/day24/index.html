About the data

About the data

Balancing authorities: the organizations that keep electricity supply and demand matched, minute by minute, in their own part of the grid. 60 of them in the Lower 48 report to the Energy Information Administration on Form EIA-930. The map also shows 7 Canadian operators and Mexico's, which appear only in their U.S. neighbors' reports.

Period and units: the 720 hours of September 2026 in Coordinated Universal Time. Each hourly value is the average power across a tie in megawatts, so the values add up to energy. Totals are in gigawatt-hours (1 GWh is 1,000 megawatt-hours).

What an arrow is: the net over the whole month between two directly connected authorities. 153 ties are drawn. A net hides back-and-forth: 78 of them ran in their net direction in at least 9 of every 10 hours, and the rest switched more often.

Direction: in the source, a positive value is electricity flowing out of the reporting authority and a negative value is electricity flowing in.

When the two sides disagree: both authorities on a tie report it, and their numbers should mirror each other. Often they do not. Hour by hour, the map uses the average of the two reports, or the one report when only one side filed (always the case for Canada and Mexico). Over the month the two sides' totals differ by about 10% of the energy drawn on two-sided ties, and on 2 ties they disagree about the direction. 0.64% of hourly reports were missing.

What it is not: the arrows are not transmission lines and do not follow any route. The values are reported interchange, as the authorities filed it, without the Administration's corrections. Jointly owned power plants and accounting arrangements between neighbors can make a tie look larger than the power that physically crossed.

What averaging does not fix: reported interchange can include accounting adjustments. Averaging makes the two sides of a tie consistent; it does not reconcile each authority's overall balance. For example, PJM's mapped ties imply about 2,668 GWh net outflow, against 778 GWh in the total it reports separately.

Positions: most authorities sit at the center of the counties their utilities serve, weighted by electricity sales (EIA-861, 2025). 18 were placed by hand, including generation-only authorities and those outside the United States, and overlapping ones were moved apart by up to 31 km. Mexico's operator is drawn twice, once for its tie with California and once for its tie with Texas. Hover a circle to see how it was placed.
