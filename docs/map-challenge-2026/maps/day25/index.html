The Texas grid as a transit diagram

58 of the state's 345 kV substations, drawn as stations on 9 lines. Two stations are joined where the source data has 345 kV transmission lines running between them. It is not to scale, and it leaves out most of the grid.

About the data

Source: transmission lines and substations from the federal HIFLD open data, which was retired and is frozen: lines as of September 2024, substations as of April 2022. Lines marked inactive are left out. Nothing built since then is here, and the source's gaps and errors are repeated.

Stations: picked by hand. In the source, 549 lines at 345 kV touch Texas and the largest connected group of them has 171 named substations in the state. These 58 were chosen to include the places where the most lines meet and to reach each part of the state. Names are the source's own, in reading case; Calaveras 345 Switchyard is shortened to Calaveras.

Links: two neighboring stations are joined only when the source has a chain of 345 kV lines between them that passes through no other station on the diagram. 34 of the 66 links are a single line in the source. The other 32 run through one to 3 substations that are not shown. Together the links follow 115 of the source's lines. The source marks 91 of those as in service and gives no status for the other 24.

Lines and colors: a drawing device, as on a subway map. They are not circuit names, owners or operating paths, and the line names are made up for this page.

What it can't tell you: distances, directions and positions are not to scale. A missing link does not mean two stations are unconnected, and a drawn link says only that the source has lines between them. Do not use it to judge how power flows or what is connected to what.

Data: HIFLD transmission lines (September 2024) and substations (April 2022), archived copies.
