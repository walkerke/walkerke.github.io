About the data

About the data

Night lights: NASA's Black Marble night-time images from the NOAA-20 satellite, sampled on 15 dates between Nov 16, 2025 and Nov 15, 2026 and averaged. NASA fills cloudy pixels with the latest clear view, so these are not 15 separate looks at every spot. Each pixel is about 500 m across. The images stop at a fixed brightness, so the brightest places are understated. Read it as a picture, not a measurement.

Heat: night-time detections from the VIIRS 375 m active fire product on the NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 satellites, Nov 16, 2025 to Nov 15, 2026. Detections within 750 m of one another are linked, and each linked group is counted as one place, so a place can hold more than one flare or plant. Its night count is the number of nights with heat anywhere in it. Counted this way, 955 places had heat on 3 or more nights, 836 of them inside the Permian Basin. Splitting the widest groups would make that 913.

Fires: some large, short-lived groups likely to be spreading fires are filtered out. Smaller wildfires and prescribed burns may remain.

Permian Basin: the outline is the U.S. Energy Information Administration's.

What it can't tell you: the satellite sees heat, not what is burning. In an oil field much of it is gas flares, but gas plants, refineries, other industry, wildfires and prescribed burns show up too. Small or cooler heat sources can go undetected, and clouds can hide heat, so a place may have burned on more nights than counted. Nothing here measures how much gas was burned.

Day: Mapbox satellite imagery. Imagery and data provided by services from NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS), part of NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System.
