About the data

About the data

Wells: well records published by state oil and gas agencies, compiled by Clearfork Intelligence in a file built on September 2, 2026. It holds 5,061,676 records from 32 states: Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Florida, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Mexico, New York, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Virginia, West Virginia and Wyoming. States not in the file are blank on the map, which does not mean they have no wells.

What is counted: 4,045,912 wells and wellbores. A record counts unless the state's own type, status or list marks it as a permit, or as a location that was canceled, expired or never drilled. That leaves out 471,290 records. Another 8,301 records marked as a permit or a location are counted anyway, because they carry a depth and a spud or completion date. State records also include a small number of other wells, such as water wells, geothermal wells and geological test holes, and these are counted too. In Missouri, for example, 38 counted wells are typed as not related to oil or gas.

Not counted, not ruled out: 64,410 records are left out because the record does not settle whether a well exists. In Oklahoma, 45,057 are coded "no evidence of well" and 885 are coded "new drill", which does not say how the drilling turned out. 18,285 in Kentucky are coded as a location, which Kentucky uses for new permits and for old wells with missing completion information. 20 in Virginia are on the state's list of pending wells. In another 163, the record contradicts itself and the details are not good enough evidence to settle it: a permit status beside a placeholder depth or a date that records a status change, a spud date on a record marked never drilled, or a Kansas note that the well was not drilled.

How repeat records are grouped: a well is counted once by its API number (3,378,603 wells and wellbores) or, where the file has none, by the state's own well or permit number (317,147, most of them in Pennsylvania, Indiana and Kansas). In Kansas, repeat operations on the same API wellbore count once, and a separately numbered sidetrack counts separately. The other 351,417 records have neither, so records at the same location are grouped and each of the 350,162 groups is counted once. Most are in Texas. A group can hold more than one well. Also left out: 17,191 records with no usable coordinates and 462,873 repeat records of a well already counted.

Hexagons: the H3 grid, in five sizes, from a typical 4,935 square miles when zoomed out to about 2.1 when zoomed in. Each well is counted in the one hexagon it falls in, and every size adds up to the same 4,045,912 wells.

Color: wells per square mile of the whole hexagon, on the same scale at every zoom. Coastal and boundary cells include their full area; larger hexagons average over more empty space. The densest places only show their full color when you zoom in.

What it can't tell you: a well on record is not a well in use. The count includes producing and idle wells, injection wells, dry holes and wells plugged decades ago. States keep different records going back different lengths of time, and code permits differently, so comparisons across state lines are rough.
