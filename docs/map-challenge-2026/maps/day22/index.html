About the data

About the data

Period and units: calendar year 2025, in billion cubic feet of gas. EIA recorded 5,509 billion cubic feet of U.S. LNG exports, about 15.1 a day.

Source: the U.S. Energy Information Administration's LNG exports by point of exit and natural gas exports by country, annual series released October 30, 2026. The first table pairs each point of exit with each destination country. The second gives the country totals, which the pairs add up to within rounding.

What is drawn: 210 lines, one for each pair of a terminal and a country with a volume in 2025, from 9 terminals to 40 countries. Each line ends in the middle of the country, not at a port. One point of exit in the table, Altamira, is in Mexico: the gas is piped there from the United States and liquefied offshore. Its 27 billion cubic feet are part of EIA's U.S. total and are drawn from Altamira.

Not drawn: 1.3 billion cubic feet, 0.02% of the total: small shipments from Fort Lauderdale and West Palm Beach, Florida, and truck crossings into Canada. With those, LNG from the United States reached 44 countries.

Mexico: This map shows LNG. In 2025, the United States also exported 2,423 billion cubic feet of natural gas to Mexico by pipeline.

Projection: Equal Earth, which keeps the areas of countries in proportion. Each line is a great circle, the shortest path over the globe, so it bends on a flat map, and lines to East Asia leave one edge and come back on the other. The lines are trade connections. They are not the routes ships took.

Where U.S. LNG went in 2025

EIA recorded 5,509 billion cubic feet of U.S. liquefied natural gas exports in 2025.
