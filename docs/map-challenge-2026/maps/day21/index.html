About the data

About the data

OpenStreetMap: every way tagged man_made=pipeline inside Texas, from OpenStreetMap data through November 10, 2026: 56,664 ways and 108,884 miles, cut at the state line and the shoreline. Color follows the substance tag. 16,691 miles carry no substance tag. 48,186 of the ways were last edited in 2022. Lines mapped only with an abandoned or disused tag are not included. 84 ways (280 miles) carry the pipeline tag along with an abandoned or disused tag; they are drawn, and their tooltips say so. © OpenStreetMap contributors, Open Database License.

Railroad Commission: the Commission's pipeline layer as downloaded on June 22, 2026 and compiled by Clearfork Intelligence: 498,369 segments and 365,060 miles, of which 315,730 miles are in service and 49,330 are abandoned or removed. It includes some offshore lines.

Why they differ: the two are made in different ways. The Commission's layer is the state's own published pipeline map. OpenStreetMap is drawn and tagged by volunteers, and a line there need not be split, classed or even traced the same way. The totals describe each source. They are not a score of one against the other.

One place the difference shows: OpenStreetMap has 49,866 miles tagged as transmission lines, 30,609 tagged as gathering lines and 28,332 with no usage tag. The Commission classes 77,638 in-service miles as transmission, 187,621 as gathering and 50,471 as other system types. The two sets of classes are not defined the same way.

Reading the map: at wide zooms both sides leave out short lines. The Commission's side drops its shortest lines where they are dense so the map can load, and OpenStreetMap's side drops lines too short to draw at that scale. Zooming in reveals more detail, though some very short OpenStreetMap ways remain too small to draw.
