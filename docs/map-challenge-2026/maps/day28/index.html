About the data

About the data

Energy burden: the average yearly cost of home energy divided by the average yearly household income. Home energy is electricity, gas (piped or bottled) and other fuels such as heating oil and wood. It does not include gasoline or water.

Source: the U.S. Department of Energy's Low-Income Energy Affordability Data (LEAD) Tool, 2022 Update. It is built from the Census Bureau's 2018-2022 American Community Survey household records and adjusted to match what electric and gas utilities reported to the Energy Information Administration for 2022. Dollars are 2022 dollars.

These are estimates, not bills: the survey does not sample enough households to measure a single tract. Average costs and incomes for each kind of household come from a larger survey area and are applied to the tract's own mix of housing. Two neighboring tracts can differ because their housing differs, not because anyone's bill was read. See the methodology.

How an area's number is made: from its totals, for all households together. Across the 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico, energy costs average $2,475 a year against an average household income of $107,660, so they take 2.3% of income. For households below the federal poverty line, the group's estimated average energy cost, $2,029 a year, is 19.6% of its average income, $10,331. The map does not show that figure for single tracts or counties, where the group can rest on a handful of households.

What is drawn: counties when the map is zoomed out, and census tracts once you zoom in. Of 85,059 tracts, 84,001 are in color. The other 1,058 are gray, with no estimate shown: 279 have fewer than 100 households in the data, and 779 are not in it, all of them tracts with no households. Every tract in the data is drawn.

Boundaries: tract and county boundaries are from the 2018-2022 American Community Survey, the release the LEAD Tool is built on, through the tidycensus R package. The Census Bureau data supply only those shapes and the tract household counts used above. Every cost, income and household count behind a burden is from LEAD.

Energy bills as a share of household income

Electricity, gas and other home fuels take 2.3% of household income across the United States and Puerto Rico, by U.S. Department of Energy estimates.
