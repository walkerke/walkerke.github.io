About the data

About the data

Ethanol plants: the U.S. Energy Information Administration's Biofuels Plant Production Capacity Report, released September 24, 2026: the 189 fuel ethanol plants operating on January 1, 2026. Capacity is EIA's estimate of how much fuel ethanol a plant is able to make in a year, in millions of gallons. It is not what the plant made. The Midwest is the region EIA calls PADD 2, from the Dakotas to Ohio and south to Oklahoma and Tennessee; it holds 175 of the plants and 94.5% of the capacity.

Why corn: in the United States nearly all fuel ethanol is made from corn kernel starch, according to EIA.

Where plants are drawn: the report gives no coordinates. 181 plants are drawn at the plant site from EIA's own map of ethanol plants as of 2021. The other 8 are drawn at the center of the city EIA lists for them, because they have no 2021 point, or because the 2021 point is a coordinate error: more than 100 km from that city, or, for one plant, checked against a state facility record and found to be about 30 km off.

Corn: the U.S. Department of Agriculture's Cropland Data Layer for 2025, which classifies land cover from satellite images. The map shows the share of each 1.5 km square classified as corn; squares under 3% are left clear. It is a picture of where corn was grown, not an acreage count.

Acres: the planted acreage in the text is the official estimate from USDA's National Agricultural Statistics Service for 2025.

What it can't tell you: where any plant buys its corn, or how much of the corn near it becomes fuel. Corn is also grown for feed, food and export.

Corn into fuel

The United States had 189 fuel ethanol plants at the start of 2026, with the capacity to make 18.4 billion gallons a year. 175 of them are in the Midwest. Gold shows land in corn in 2025, when farmers planted 98.7 million acres of it. 189 U.S. fuel ethanol plants, sized by capacity, over land in corn in 2025.
